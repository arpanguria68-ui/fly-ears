Senses and commands

How to read this pagewhat is measured, what is modelled

Measured from the simulation (every number, spike, raster mark and bar): the spiking of the real MaleCNS wiring, 166,700 neurons, one 20 ms step at a time, in the video's own time. audit.py re-runs it and gets the same numbers step for step; a blank grey view in silence stays at rest.
Modelled by this program, then injected : light goes into the photoreceptors (brightness per eye column; R8 green, R7 blue); L2/L3 lamina cells get the darkening at their column, as real ones signal it, because this spiking model cannot pass the photoreceptors' graded inhibition on; the ears get 8 frequency bands, JO-B low and JO-A high (an assumption); smells and tastes go into their receptors. The connectome does the rest.
Drawn, not simulated: the fly's body is a picture posed by its motor neurons (no muscles or physics, no wingbeat: real wings beat 200 times a second); "emotion" words name populations, not feelings. Scales are fixed for every video: a full bar = +10 spikes/neuron/s, an emotion bar = +-5, and a state is named only at +1 or more. Brain map: up to 3,000 of the spikes each frame.

What else a real fly has

Sensesstimulus on = red light

Spikes per receptor neuron per second. Smell, taste, wind, temperature, humidity and touch are given on the Watch page's schedule; colour comes from the video (R7 mostly sees ultraviolet, which video has none of: blue stands in).

Smell and memory

Receptors -> projection neurons -> mushroom body (Kenyon cells, the learning centre) and lateral horn (innate) -> memory output neurons.

Compasscentral complex

The 50 EPG neurons of the ellipsoid body ring, at their real places around it; lit = fired this frame. Their bump is the fly's sense of heading.

Clock and hunger

Circadian clock neurons (LNv, LNd, DN1) and the insulin-producing cells (fed state).
